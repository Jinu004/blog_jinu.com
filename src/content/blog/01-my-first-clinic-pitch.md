For weeks I had a plan to walk into a clinic and talk about what I am building. For weeks I found reasons not to. I told myself the pitch was not ready, or that I needed one more feature first. The honest reason was nerves.

Then one afternoon I did it. I walked into a small clinic in Kochi to pitch a WhatsApp booking assistant: something that answers patient messages and books appointments, so the front desk does not have to do it all by hand.

## What happened

I spoke to the front desk staff first and asked if I could see the doctor. I was told the doctor had gone for lunch. I took that as a polite way of saying "not now", and I think that was the right way to read it.

So I talked to the staff instead, and that turned out to be the useful part. I asked how patients book appointments. They told me patients mostly book by phone, because a call is quicker. WhatsApp booking is supported, but the staff reply to those messages by hand. The clinic looked quiet that afternoon.

I left my visiting card and did not push. Nobody there owed me their time, and pushing would only have made a second visit harder.

## The real win

I did not sign anyone up. I did not get to speak to the doctor. If you measure the visit by results, there were none.

But the real win was taking the first step. I had put this off for weeks. Walking in, saying what I do and walking out having learned something was exactly the part I was scared of. Now it is done once, and I expect the next visit to be easier because of it.

## What I learned

Patients call because calling is fast. That sounds obvious, but hearing it from the people who answer the phone made it land differently. A patient does not care which channel the clinic prefers. They use whatever gets them an appointment quickest.

So the shift to WhatsApp will not happen because WhatsApp is modern, or because it suits the clinic. It will only happen when booking there is as fast as a call. If a patient sends a message and then has to wait until someone at the desk is free to reply by hand, the phone wins.

That is the bar I have to clear. Not "nice to have", but as fast as picking up the phone.

## What I will do differently

Next time I will ask two simple questions: how many patients does the clinic see in a day, and how many calls come in? I left without either number, and without them I cannot tell how much phone booking really costs a clinic like this one.

## A question for clinic owners

If you run a clinic, I would like to hear from you: how do your patients book today?
